8.1 – The navigations of this site

8.1 – The navigations of this site

13. Sept. 20266 Min. Lesezeit

Three navigations are visible on this page: the chapters in the left column, “In this chapter” in the right one and the previous/next links below the text. On phones a menu behind the burger button joins them. In the configuration they are five entries: chapter list, menu and pager for both languages, the chapter tree per language.

menueaccordionburger menu, phones onlythe page’s

How the languages are laid out here

The German pages are in the root, the English ones under en/; quartz-multilanguage recognises English by the folder and gives every other page the default language, German. From that the navigation builds one tree per language: the German one holds only the German pages, the English one only the English pages, and without the folder en — en/1-getting-started appears in it as 1-getting-started. How this works for every layout is in 8.3.

The pager

One entry for both languages. With the default language: auto the pager pages through the tree of the language the page is written in: after the last German page nothing follows, not the English start page.

The words come out right by themselves. “Previous” and “Next” on English pages, “Zurück” and “Weiter” on German ones, and the name for screen readers likewise (7.3). Neither labels: false nor an ariaLabel is needed.

Despite priority: 5, the pager appears below the backlinks, as the last thing before the footer. The backlinks belong to the custom-8 group, and this website’s layout frame puts that group into an area of its own above the rest of afterBody. priority only orders within one area.

The chapter tree

The German entry differs in language: de, in title and id.

  • Two entries, because title is text. The navigation translates the plugin’s words, but not a heading of your own. Each language therefore gets its entry with a fixed language.
  • language: en always shows the English tree. On a German page the page is not in it, scope: section finds no chapter, and the entry does not appear (3.1). In the same way kapitel-de disappears on English pages.
  • scope: section shows the chapter the page is in. The start page has none, so the top of the column stays empty there. The chapter of an English page is 1-getting-started, not en, because the English tree does not contain the language folder.
  • tree rather than accordion, because a chapter has three or four pages — nothing to expand.
  • showScopeRoot puts the chapter name above as a link.
  • title is the heading, and at the same time the name for screen readers.
  • mobile: hidden, because on phones the pager below the text is enough.
  • breakpoints: 900px in all five entries, because this website’s layout frame wraps at 900 pixels and not at the 800 from variables.scss (6.2).

All five entries share the same tree-shaping options. The build therefore computes two trees, one per language, not five (1.3).

The chapter list instead of the explorer

  • One entry for both languages. Without a title there is no text of its own, and with language: auto the list shows the nine German chapters on German pages and the nine English ones on English pages.
  • depth: 1 shows only the chapters, not their pages. The work is shared: the left says where you can go, the right where you are within the chapter. A whole tree on the left would show the pages of the current chapter twice. The chapter you are reading is marked.
  • showHome puts “Home” in front, “Start” on German pages — each time the start page of the page’s own language (3.2).
  • No folderClick: with depth: 1 there is nothing to expand; every row is a link to the chapter page.
  • mobile: hidden, because the menu takes over on phones.

The menu on phones

On phones the right column is gone; “In this chapter” is hidden there. So that every page stays reachable through the menu, it shows more than the chapter list — and is therefore an entry of its own, because depth applies at every width:

  • accordion with every page. The chapter of the current page is open, the page marked (5.1).
  • exclusive keeps one chapter open at a time; nine open chapters would be 45 rows.
  • folderClick: link: the chapter name leads to the chapter page, the arrow next to it expands.
  • mobile: offcanvas puts the menu behind a burger button.

Phones only, and where the explorer was

The plugin has no “phones only” mode. The menu is therefore hidden on desktops by a file of its own, through the class from className. The same file puts the burger in the right place: this website’s template is cut to the explorer; below 900 pixels the header keeps a gap on the left for its button, and the left column has no height there. Left alone, the burger would sit on top of the breadcrumbs.

The file is quartz/styles/custom/plugin-navigations.scss, included in custom.scss next to the template’s other files:

  • not all and (max-width: 900px) is the exact negation of the plugin’s query. min-width: 901px would leave a window 900.5 pixels wide without either entry (6.2).
  • The --tpl-* variables belong to the template. The burger thereby sits where the explorer’s button used to sit and stays visible above the open menu; a second tap closes it, which is why the ✕ goes (6.1).
  • Also in the file: the drawer’s background, rounding and shadow, so it looks like the explorer’s.

What is not switched on here

stripNumericPrefix. The titles of this website start with their number — “8.1 – The navigations …” — and the option would take “8.” off (2.1). Sorting is still right: by the numbers of the file and folder names, 01-…, 02-….
